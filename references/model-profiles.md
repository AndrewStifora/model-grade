# Model profiles for routing

Facts distilled from Anthropic's prompt-engineering pages, model pages, the Effort page, Choosing the right model, and Optimizing for cost and intelligence (fetched 2026-11-15; URLs and hashes in `sources.md` and `sources.json`). Prices are first-party API list rates per million tokens. "Alias" is what the Claude Code `--model` flag and the Agent tool accept. Quoted sentences are verbatim from the docs; `scripts/check_quotes.py` checks them against the cached pages.

Relative price per token: Sonnet (5.5 and 5) = 1x, Haiku 5.5 = 0.05x (0.25x on prompts over 100K tokens), Opus 5.5 = 2x, Opus 5 = 2.5x, Fable 5.2 = 4x. Cache reads cost 2.5% of the input price on Fable 5.2, 5% on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and 10% on the rest, so a warm cache is worth more on the expensive models. Per-token price does not predict cost per completed task: "Compare models on cost per completed task, not per token, because you pay for completed tasks."

In Claude Code the effort default differs from the API's: Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, and Haiku 5.5 all start at medium there. A family alias (`opus`, `sonnet`, `haiku`, `fable`) passed to a subagent resolves to the session's own model when the session is on a model of that family, so a `--run` on `fable` from a Fable 5.1 session runs Fable 5.1.

Claude Haiku 4.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 are legacy and are not routed to. Haiku 5.5 replaces Haiku 4.5 at a tenth of its price; Fable 5.2 replaces Fable 5.1 at four fifths of its price with the same classifiers, so neither old model covers a gap the fallbacks below do not.

## Tier 0: Claude Haiku 5.5

- "Claude Haiku 5.5 is built for high-volume, latency-sensitive work such as classification, routing, extraction, and subagent tasks." The model-selection matrix lists it for "Real-time applications, high-volume intelligent processing, cost-sensitive deployments needing strong reasoning, subagent tasks", and the cost guide says "Claude Haiku 5.5 suits high-volume and latency-sensitive work with checkable outputs."
- Measured: on GPQA Diamond at its default effort it reached "85% accuracy, compared with 91% for each of Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5", at "About a twentieth of Claude Opus 5.5's cost per correct answer and about a tenth of Claude Sonnet 5.5's". On a set of 79 Claude Managed Agents tasks the move from Haiku 4.5 took the pass rate "From 62% to 93% of attempts passed, one run per model", at about $0.011 per passed attempt. No coding benchmark is published for it.
- Efficiency-first is a documented way to choose: "For many applications, starting with a faster, more cost-effective model such as Claude Haiku 5.5 can be the optimal approach", upgrading "only if necessary for specific capability gaps".
- Effort: "Start with `medium` for most work, including agentic coding. Use `low`, the cheapest and fastest level, for chat, short tool tasks, and simple, high-volume requests. In long agent prompts, the model is more likely to skip a search, stop early, or skip a check at `low`. Use `high` for knowledge work, longer agent tasks, and strict instruction following. Use `xhigh` or `max` only where your evals show a quality gain, and compare them with Claude Sonnet 5.5 on performance, cost, and speed."
- Behaviors to pass on: "At `low` and `medium` effort, Claude Haiku 5.5 sometimes reports a code change as done without running a check" (the docs give a verification paragraph). With a search tool, give it today's date; at low effort it may skip a search. "Claude Haiku 5.5 sometimes writes reasoning-like text in the reply that users see. This occurs more often with thinking off or at `low` effort."
- Constraints: safety classifiers decline in four categories: `cyber` ("Finding vulnerabilities in source code is allowed. High-risk dual-use cybersecurity work isn't allowed. Benign cybersecurity work can also trigger this category."), `bio` ("Everyday health and educational questions aren't affected."), `frontier_llm`, and `general_harms` ("Benign work can also trigger this category."). "Claude Haiku 5.5 has no server-side fallback", and "Sending the same request to Claude Haiku 5.5 again usually returns another refusal." A prompt over 100K tokens is billed at five times the base rate, input and output. Manual `budget_tokens`, sampling parameters, assistant prefill, and the `computer_20250124` tool are rejected. Unlike every other current model it still accepts a forced `tool_choice` (the response then starts with the tool call, with no thinking) and `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` at effort high or below.
- Claude Code: the `haiku` alias resolves to Haiku 5.5 on the Anthropic API from v2.1.293; earlier versions resolve it to Haiku 4.5, which has no effort levels.
- Fit: classification, extraction, routing, simple transforms, short tool tasks, chat, subagent work handed a narrow brief, and the cheap first attempt at a small, well-specified task whose result a checker will verify.
- Do not use for: unverified answers where six points of accuracy matter, long agentic loops nobody watches, or dense charts and screenshots (no docs claim covers them).

## Tier 1: Claude Sonnet 5.5

- "Claude Sonnet 5.5 offers the best combination of speed and intelligence." It is "built for everyday coding, agent, and enterprise workloads, such as code generation, data analysis, content creation, visual understanding, and agentic tool use". Same price as Sonnet 5, 1M context, 128K output. "Existing Claude Sonnet 5 prompts should perform well without changes."
- Measured against Sonnet 5 on the SWE-bench Pro subset at default effort: "About 10 more points" for "87% less per solved task". At its default it solved 87.6% for $0.11 per solved task.
- Where it stops: "For the hardest long-horizon work, an Opus model is the better choice."
- Effort: "Effort levels are recalibrated. An effort level doesn't produce the same amount of thinking as it did on Claude Sonnet 5." "Start at `high` unless your workload is agentic or latency-sensitive. For agentic coding and multistep tool use, start at `medium` for well-specified tasks and move to `high` for harder or longer ones. For chat and other latency-sensitive work, start at `medium` or `low`." Medium is a real trade on coding: on the SWE-bench Pro subset it scored about 7 points below high for about 59% of the cost per task, and on an internal agentic-coding benchmark 62.7% against 77.8%.
- Low effort on agentic work: "At `low`, it keeps its thinking short and can skip verifying a change." "At `low` and `medium`, on long agentic tasks, it's more likely to stop and check in with the user before it completes its work." The docs give a system-prompt paragraph for each (run a real check before reporting done; keep working until everything asked for is done).
- Reasoning with JSON output (the shape of a grading or classification call): "On tasks like these, the model often answers without thinking first, particularly at `low` and `medium` effort." Adding "Think the problem through before you answer." at `high` "brings accuracy close to what the model reaches at `xhigh`".
- Scope: it "tends to add tests, documentation, and small supporting files that fit your repository's conventions, even when you don't ask for them". At `xhigh` and `max` it "can start its own rounds of review" after it finishes a task; the docs' stop-and-report instruction keeps that work on the task.
- Tools and vision: in chat and knowledge work it "sometimes answers from its training knowledge when a web search would catch details that have changed", so tell it to search for specifics. For dense charts, "with tools at `high` effort, the model read charts more accurately than without tools at `max` effort, at a fraction of the cost".
- Constraints: safety classifiers can decline in five categories: `cyber` ("Finding vulnerabilities in source code is allowed. High-risk dual-use cybersecurity work isn't allowed."), `bio` ("Everyday health and educational questions aren't affected."), `frontier_llm`, `reasoning_extraction`, and `general_harms`. Server-side fallback retries `cyber` and `frontier_llm` declines on Claude Sonnet 5 and no other category. Forced `tool_choice` (`any` or `tool`) returns a 400. `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}` returns a 400; `between_tools` replaces it but is rejected at `xhigh` and `max`. The `computer_20251124` tool is not accepted on the Claude API and Google Cloud. Thinking blocks are bound to the model and the conversation; of the routed models only Opus 5.5 (on the Claude API) and Fable 5.2 read them.
- Claude Code: the `sonnet` alias resolves to Sonnet 5.5 on the Anthropic API from v2.1.284; earlier versions resolve it to Sonnet 5. Pin with the full ID or the `ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_SONNET_MODEL` environment variable.
- Fit: bounded coding tasks (one file to a few files) with clear acceptance criteria, scripts, unit tests, summaries, drafting, research and comparison memos, extraction that must be right without a check, interactive coding and chat, ordinary screenshots, and chart reading when it has a crop or zoom tool.

## Tier 1 fallback: Claude Sonnet 5

Keep Sonnet 5 only for work the current models cannot take. It costs the same per token as Sonnet 5.5, is legacy ("you should consider migrating to Claude Sonnet 5.5"), and carries no classifiers.

- "Claude Sonnet 5 has particular strengths in coding and agentic tasks."
- "Claude Sonnet 5 is more agentic than Claude Sonnet 4.6 by default and reaches for tools and runs self-verification loops more readily."
- Effort: "At `low` and `medium`, the model scopes its work to what was asked rather than going beyond the request. This is good for latency and cost, but on moderately complex tasks running at `low` effort there is some risk of under-thinking." Medium is "Comparable to Claude Sonnet 4.6 at high effort", and xhigh is "For the hardest coding and agentic tasks."
- Constraints: no mid-conversation system messages; `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k` are rejected. No safety classifiers are documented for it, which makes it the last-resort model for legitimate work that the classifiers on Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus, and Fable decline.
- When to pick it: work a current model's classifiers may decline (dual-use security, biology or life science, development of competing AI models, or a `general_harms` false positive), at any tier where the work is within its reach; integrations that force `tool_choice`, send `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`, or use `computer_20251124` on the Claude API or Google Cloud and need more than Haiku 5.5; and API harnesses that edit earlier turns, which Sonnet 5 does not check.

## Tier 2: Claude Opus 5.5

- "Claude Opus 5.5 is built for long-running agentic coding and knowledge work. Typical uses include multihour autonomous coding agents, large-scale refactoring, complex systems engineering, vision-heavy workflows, and computer use." It is the documented starting point: "Most workloads start with Claude Opus 5.5", and "For most agent workloads, start with Claude Opus 5.5 at its default effort (`medium`)."
- "Claude Opus 5.5 generates output tokens more than 30 percent faster than Claude Opus 5 and tends to complete the same task with fewer tokens." Against Opus 5 on the SWE-bench Pro subset it scores about the same for "78% less per solved task".
- Agentic coding and review: "The model is strongest on multistep work in a real repository, such as carrying a change through a large code base until its tests pass. In Anthropic's testing, at its default `medium` effort the model matched or beat Claude Opus 5 at `high` effort on such tasks, in fewer steps and with fewer tokens. It also sustains long-running autonomous work better than Claude Opus 5, such as multihour audits and migrations of large code bases run end to end with parallel subagents and little oversight. Early testers also reported stronger code review, with more bugs caught than on Claude Opus 5 and fewer false alarms".
- Knowledge work: "The model is much less likely to state an incorrect figure or cite the wrong source." On DeepResearch Bench II at default effort, "Claude Opus 5.5 scored 2.4 points above Claude Sonnet 5.5 for 1.5 times the cost per task."
- Vision and computer use: "even at its lowest effort setting it read values off dense charts more accurately than Claude Opus 5 did at its highest, using a small fraction of the output tokens." Without tools, raising effort improves its reading of technical drawings "but does little for charts".
- Effort, measured on the SWE-bench Pro subset (tasks solved, cost per task): low 87.4% for $0.11, medium 92.8% for $0.21, high 95.3% for $0.29, xhigh 96.7% for $0.74. With a checker, low with the failures re-run at high solved about 97% for about $0.17: "At `low`, 13% of tasks failed. With those re-run at `high`, the pass rate was slightly higher than with everything at `high`, for a little over half the cost."
- Low is not safe everywhere. "Claude Opus 5.5 scored 13.3 points lower at `low` than at `medium` on DRACO", a research benchmark, and on an internal defect-search benchmark "Claude Opus 5.5 scored 34 points lower at `low` than at `medium`, for about a fifth of the cost." Keep research, analysis, and bug hunting at medium or above.
- Behaviors to know: it "tends to get to work quickly"; on loosely specified multi-app tasks, an instruction to explore the connected apps before acting raised completion noticeably. On unattended runs it may end a turn with a report instead of the next tool call; a standing instruction in the docs addresses that. At `xhigh` and `max`, set a large `max_tokens`.
- Constraints: "The model runs a biology safety classifier in addition to the cybersecurity one", and requests that push it to reproduce its reasoning can be declined as `reasoning_extraction`. Finding vulnerabilities in source code is allowed; high-risk dual-use cybersecurity work is not. On the 50-task DeepResearch Bench II set, "safeguards nearly always stopped it on four biology tasks that Claude Sonnet 5.5 answered". Thinking cannot be disabled (400). Forced `tool_choice` (`any` or `tool`) returns a 400. The older `computer_20251124` tool type is not supported on the Claude API and Google Cloud. Thinking blocks are bound to the model and the conversation; it reads Haiku 5.5's and Sonnet 5.5's on the Claude API, and Fable 5.2 reads its blocks.
- Claude Code: the `opus` alias resolves to Opus 5.5 from Claude Code v2.1.280 (`claude update`); earlier versions resolve it to Opus 5. Pin with the full ID or the `ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_OPUS_MODEL` environment variable.
- Fit: the default for real agent workloads, multi-file coding, multi-hour autonomous migrations and audits, code review, dense charts and screenshots, computer use, long-context analysis, financial models and office deliverables, multi-app workflow automation, and research where the figures and sources must be right.

## Tier 2 fallback: Claude Opus 5

Keep Opus 5 only for work Opus 5.5 cannot take. It costs 25% more per token and several times more per solved task.

- "Claude Opus 5 is strongest on difficult coding tasks: multifile features, larger refactors, and end-to-end feature work."
- Effort: "Start with `high`, the default", and "Use `low` and `medium` liberally as your primary control for token cost and response time wherever your evals show quality holds."
- When to pick it instead of 5.5: biology or life-science work (no biology classifier is documented for Opus 5); high-risk dual-use security work that 5.5 declines (Opus 5 has the cybersecurity classifier too, so it is not guaranteed); integrations that must run with thinking disabled, force `tool_choice`, or use the `computer_20251124` tool type, when the work needs more than Sonnet 5; and API harnesses that edit earlier turns, which Opus 5 does not check. It is also one of Fable 5.2's two permitted refusal-fallback targets.

## Tier 3: Claude Fable 5.2

- "Claude Fable 5.2 is built for demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work. Typical uses include agent sessions that run for hours, multistep deep research, and analysis carried through to a finished document, spreadsheet, or deck." It is "Anthropic's most capable model open to all customers".
- It is not the starting point. Its own page says to "move to Claude Fable 5.2 when your evals on a Claude Opus model at higher effort still don't meet your requirements", and the cost guide says "Use Claude Fable 5.2 for demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work, or when your evals on Claude Opus 5.5 at higher effort still fall short."
- Measured on coding (463 problems of the SWE-bench Pro subset): at high it solved 96.3% for $1.39 per solved task. "At `low`, Fable 5.2 solved 94.6% for $0.39 USD per solved task: more tasks than Fable 5.1 at its default, for about a third of the cost." Opus 5.5 is cheaper at a similar pass rate: 95.5% for $0.30 at high, and "at `xhigh` 96.5% for $0.75 USD, level with Fable 5.2 at `high` for a little over half the cost."
- Measured on research (DeepResearch Bench II, default effort): "Claude Fable 5.2 cost 4.3 times as much as Claude Sonnet 5.5 for no measurable gain in score, and 2.9 times as much as Claude Opus 5.5 for a score 1.9 points lower." It also scored 4.7 to 7.0 points below Fable 5.1 there. The earlier rule that Fable at low wins research on cost per task does not hold for Fable 5.2.
- Effort: start at high. "You get most of the capability gain when using `high` effort." "Above `high`, gains are uneven and task-dependent." "`low` suits quick back-and-forth, well-specified tasks, and high-volume workloads (intelligent and fast subagents, for example). On coding evaluations, Claude Fable 5.2 performs better than past models at `low` effort." "`medium` suits interactive coding, chat, and everyday knowledge work." "`high` suits hard coding, research, agentic work, and long unattended runs. Turns take noticeably longer to finish than at `medium`." "`xhigh` is for your hardest long-running coding and agentic work where you've measured a gain. It raised scores slightly on Anthropic's internal coding evaluations and used many more tokens." "`max` is for measuring the model's ceiling or for the rare task where time and tokens don't matter." On research, "Claude Fable 5.2 scored 3.3 points higher at `high` than at `low` on DeepResearch Bench II, for 3.2 times the cost, and not measurably higher at `medium` than at `low`".
- Behaviors to pass on: "On long, multistep tasks, Claude Fable 5.2 sometimes ends a turn before the work is done", and "This happens more often at `low` and `medium` effort"; the docs give a `# Finishing work` paragraph for agentic work at those levels. "At `high` and above, the model thinks before almost every reply, including a reply to a greeting." "Claude Fable 5.2 pays close attention to who owns the code it works on": without an ownership line it adds to the existing structure instead of restructuring. It follows style rules and cautions closely, so stale "never" rules in memory or instruction files hold it back.
- Orchestration: "Early testers had good results with Claude Fable 5.2 as the planner and orchestrator for other models", but "subagents can account for a large part of what a session costs, so check which model your subagents use by default." "Claude Fable 5.2 works well on long-running asynchronous tasks, so Anthropic recommends the model for work where nobody needs frequent updates."
- Constraints: safety classifiers decline in `cyber`, `bio`, `frontier_llm`, `reasoning_extraction`, and `general_harms`, and "Benign cybersecurity and life sciences work can also trigger the `cyber` and `bio` classifiers." "The permitted fallback targets for Claude Fable 5.2 are Claude Opus 4.8 and Claude Opus 5." "Claude Fable 5.2 requires 30-day data retention and isn't available under zero data retention unless expressly authorized by Anthropic." Forced `tool_choice` returns a 400, and so does the `computer_20251124` tool everywhere except Amazon Bedrock. A request that edits the system prompt, the tools, or an earlier message after a thinking block returns a 400 on every account. It reads thinking blocks from every routed model, but "No other model reads their blocks", so a move away from Fable 5.2 runs without its reasoning.
- Claude Code: the `fable` alias resolves to Fable 5.2 from v2.1.321, except in Claude apps gateway sessions; earlier versions resolve it to Fable 5.1.
- Fit: the hardest, most ambiguous, or longest-running work; problems that have resisted Opus 5.5 at high or xhigh; planning and orchestrating other models at scale; long unattended runs.

## Cross-model equivalences the docs state

- Coding (SWE-bench Pro subset, cost per solved task): Sonnet 5.5 at high 87.6% for $0.11; Opus 5.5 at low 87.4% for $0.12; Opus 5.5 at medium 92.8% for $0.22; Opus 5.5 at high about 95.5% for $0.30; Opus 5.5 at xhigh about 96.5% for $0.75; Fable 5.2 at low 94.6% for $0.39; Fable 5.2 at high 96.3% for $1.39. "Opus 5.5 at `low` solved about the same share of tasks as Sonnet 5.5 at its default, inside run-to-run noise, for about 7% more per solved task."
- Internal agentic-coding benchmark, medium effort: Opus 5.5 86.6% for $0.84 per attempt; Fable 5.2 88.9% for $1.34 per attempt (a single run). Sonnet 5.5 scored 77.8% at high and 62.7% at medium.
- Research (DeepResearch Bench II, default effort): Opus 5.5 is 2.4 points above Sonnet 5.5 for 1.5 times the cost; Fable 5.2 is not measurably above Sonnet 5.5 and is 1.9 points below Opus 5.5 at 2.9 times its cost. Fable 5.2 by effort: 54.5% at low for $1.49 a task, 55.0% at medium for $2.16, 57.7% at high for $4.82.
- Effort on Opus 5.5 outside coding: low costs 13.3 points on a research benchmark, 3.4 on HLE, 14.0 on a physics set, and 34 on defect search, against medium.
- Knowledge questions (GPQA Diamond): Haiku 5.5 at medium 85%; Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 91%, at about ten and twenty times Haiku's cost per correct answer.
- Re-run on failure (Opus 5.5, SWE-bench Pro subset): low with failures re-run at high solved about 97% for about $0.17 a task, against 95.3% for $0.29 with everything at high. "Use this policy for the saving, not the lift." It needs "a failure signal (here, the benchmark's own tests); a checker that passes bad work lets those failures through."
- Upgrades at default effort: Sonnet 5 to Sonnet 5.5 costs 87% less per solved task; Opus 5 to Opus 5.5 78% less; Haiku 4.5 to Haiku 5.5 about 84% less per passed attempt on the Managed Agents task set.
